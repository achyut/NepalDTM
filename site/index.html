What it is

Ground height, not treetop height

Free global elevation models such as Copernicus GLO-30 measure the surface: the top of the forest canopy and the roofs of buildings. In Nepal, where forests cover much of the hills and the Terai, that puts the "ground" metres too high.

NepalDTM estimates the bare earth underneath. Flood models, road and hydropower design, landslide studies, and irrigation planning all need the ground, not the canopy.

Every tile comes with a surface model, a bare-earth model, and a per-pixel error estimate, so you can see how far to trust each value.

What's new in v2

A better input surface, not a bigger model

Thirty-one recorded experiments on v1 showed that its error came from the stereo mosaic it started from: adding predictors to the ground model moved the national error by at most 2%. The stereo strips agree with each other to within a metre where they overlap, yet the mosaic built from them was offset by 1.5–2.3 m. So v2 rebuilds the input.

Adjust every strip to the laser

Each of the {{v2_n_strips}} NASA High Mountain Asia stereo strips gets its own tilted correction plane, solved together against ICESat-2 ground photons and against the strips it overlaps.

  • {{v2_n_photon_ties_m}} M photon ties
  • {{v2_n_overlap_ties_m}} M strip-to-strip ties
  • robust least squares

Keep stereo at cliffs

v1 threw away stereo cells that disagreed with Copernicus, including at cliffs where Copernicus itself is wrong. v2 keeps them wherever two adjusted strips agree.

  • HMA stereo · {{v2_share_hma}}% of area (v1 {{share_hma}}%)
  • Copernicus fill · {{v2_share_fill}}%

Retrain and test on new photons

The ground model is retrained on the new surface. Every model is scored on the same ICESat-2 photons, from whole tracks never used in the adjustment or training.

  • {{v2_n_photons_m}} M held-out photons
  • Langtang 2 m stereo check
  • Khumbu differential GPS

How it is built

Adjusted stereo, laser ground truth, one correction model

The sharpest available surface is rebuilt from adjusted stereo strips, then a model trained on laser ground returns predicts how much vegetation and buildings add to it, and subtracts that.

Build the best surface

The adjusted NASA High Mountain Asia 8 m stereo strips are stacked wherever they exist. Copernicus GLO-30 fills the gaps.

  • HMA stereo 8 m · {{v2_share_hma}}% of area
  • Copernicus GLO-30 · {{v2_share_fill}}%

Learn where the ground is

Laser altimeters on ICESat-2 and GEDI see through gaps in the canopy to the ground. A model learns the canopy and building offset from them.

  • {{n_icesat2}} ICESat-2 points
  • {{n_gedi}} GEDI points
  • canopy height · land cover · strip agreement

Remove it and test honestly

The offset is subtracted from the surface. Whole ICESat-2 tracks were held out from everything and used only for scoring.

  • {{v2_n_photons_m}} M held-out photons
  • Khumbu differential GPS
  • Langtang 2 m stereo model

Know the limits

8 m grid, not 8 m detail everywhere

The file spacing is 8 m across the whole country, but where the stereo strips have gaps the terrain detail comes from 30 m data. The aux file in every tile says which source each pixel came from.

Download and use it

Free to use with attribution under CC BY 4.0. The full datasets are archived on Zenodo with DOIs: v2.1 (doi:10.5281/zenodo.24483836) and v1.1 (doi:10.5281/zenodo.24483831). They replace the earlier v2.0 and v1.0 releases, which had rings from the gap fill. Click any point on the map to get the tile that covers it.

Cloud Optimized GeoTIFF tiles
