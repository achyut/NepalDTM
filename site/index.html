What it is

Ground height, not treetop height

Free global elevation models such as Copernicus GLO-30 measure the surface: the top of the forest canopy and the roofs of buildings. In Nepal, where forests cover much of the hills and the Terai, that puts the "ground" metres too high.

NepalDTM estimates the bare earth underneath. Flood models, road and hydropower design, landslide studies, and irrigation planning all need the ground, not the canopy.

Every tile comes with a surface model, a bare-earth model, and a per-pixel error estimate, so you can see how far to trust each value.

How it is built

Three satellite sources, one correction model

The sharpest available surface is stitched together, then a model trained on laser ground returns predicts how much vegetation and buildings add to it, and subtracts that.

Stitch the best surface

The NASA High Mountain Asia 8 m stereo mosaic is used wherever it exists. Copernicus GLO-30 fills the gaps.

  • HMA DEM 8 m · {{share_hma}}% of area
  • Copernicus GLO-30 · {{share_fill}}%

Learn where the ground is

Laser altimeters on ICESat-2 and GEDI see through gaps in the canopy to the ground. A model learns the canopy and building offset from them.

  • {{n_icesat2}} ICESat-2 points
  • {{n_gedi}} GEDI points
  • canopy height · land cover

Remove it and test honestly

The offset is subtracted from the surface. Whole 48 km blocks were left out of training and used only for scoring.

  • {{n_heldout}} held-out points
  • Khumbu differential GPS
  • Survey Department contours

Know the limits

8 m grid, not 8 m detail everywhere

The file spacing is 8 m across the whole country, but where the stereo mosaic has gaps the terrain detail comes from 30 m data. The aux file in every tile says which source each pixel came from.
